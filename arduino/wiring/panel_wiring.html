Before you power up

Do not connect

  • Switches go to GND only, never to 5 V. No resistors are needed; the Mega's internal pull-ups are switched on in the code.
  • Spinner bottom plug RX1 and TX1: leave open.
  • Spinner top plug pins 1, 2, 3, 6: leave open.
  • The spinner's 4-pin header on the right edge (3.3V / SWIM / GND / NRST) is a programming port. Leave it alone.
  • Nothing else on Mega pins 20 / 21 (no I2C devices).

Finding the spinner pins

Hold the spinner board so the 6-pin plug is at the top and the DIP switch is at the bottom right. Count the top plug's pins 1 to 6 from left to right. Pin 4 idles near 5 V; pin 5 reads 0 V until it is wired to the Mega.

Switches with three legs

If a toggle switch has three terminals, use the middle one and one outer one. Leave the other outer terminal open. If the switch reads backwards (on when the lever is down), use the other outer terminal instead.

Power and ground

  • Everything runs from the Mega's USB power. No separate supply.
  • Use a terminal block or lever connectors for the +5 V and GND rails so each device gets its own wire.
  • Optional: a 0.1 µF capacitor from the slider wiper to GND steadies the reading.

If something moves the wrong way

  • Slider runs backwards: swap the wires on its two end pins (wires 9 and 10).
  • Spinner runs backwards: swap that spinner's pin 4 and pin 5 wires.
  • Neither needs a code change.

Test after wiring

  1. Upload sketch.ino (board: Arduino Mega 2560).
  2. Open the Serial Monitor at 115200 baud. Lines look like S:1,0,1,0,0,A:0,0,512,E:3,-1.
  3. Flip each switch: the first three numbers after S: change between 0 and 1.
  4. Slide the pot: the third number after A: goes from about 0 to 1023.
  5. Turn each spinner: the numbers after E: go positive one way, negative the other.
  6. Close the Serial Monitor before starting the exhibit app.
